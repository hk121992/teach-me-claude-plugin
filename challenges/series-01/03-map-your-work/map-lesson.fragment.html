Lesson 3 · what to hand over

Map your work

Your whole recurring week on one page — what goes to the agent, what goes to a duller tool, and what stays yours on purpose.

Why put your week on one page

The skill after doing a task with the agent is choosing which tasks. A delegation map is that choice made visible: your real recurring work, sorted, each with one line of why.

And here's the freeing part: you don't have to AI everything. Some of your work belongs to a more boring tool that's right every single time — and some of it belongs to no one but you. The map steers the rest of this course, and the machine you'll build at the end of it is chosen from this map.

Four buckets

AI
work you'd give a sharp colleague
Deterministic
work a formula, template, or script could do
Hybrid
part one, part the other — split it
Human-only
yours, on purpose
Human-only is a bucket to be proud of. Relationships, accountability, calls you own — choosing not to delegate is a delegation decision in its own right, not a gap in your automation.

The rule — read this after you've sorted

Done the sort on the next slide? It lands harder that way — you'll have already used this rule without its name.

Judgment-work vs rules-work. If you can fully write down the rules, don't use the model to guess them — a formula, template, or script is rerunnable, auditable, and right every time. Keep the model for language, judgment, and mess.

Two ways to lose: over-build (a script where a template would do) and under-build (a copy-paste ritual no tool ever absorbed).

Lesson 3 · your activity

Sort your real week

Right here, in this chat with your guide — nothing to open, no new task. You do the sorting; your guide can hold the pen.

  1. List what recurs. Aim for 8 or more — the small chores count (that's where machines hide). Here are the recurring tasks that eat my week: …
  2. Sort them. Bucket each — AI / deterministic / hybrid / human-only — with one line of why. Your human-only entries need a reason you're proud of, not an apology.
  3. Say your rule. Before anyone names one for you: My rule for what goes to the agent versus a tool was: …
  4. Re-examine one bucket. Pick the bucket you're most sure of and walk one task through, start to finish. Does every step really belong there? If it moves, move it — that moment is the lesson.
  5. Split one hybrid. Name its AI part and its deterministic part, then pick the simplest rung for the deterministic part (template → checklist → formula → script → staged workflow) — and say why not a rung higher.
  6. Mark your capstone candidates. Star 1–2 recurring tasks worth a machine — the build at the end of this course is chosen from this map.
  7. Keep the map. Save my delegation map as delegation-map.md in series-01-foundations — my buckets, my reasons, my words.

Take these with you
