Lesson 7 · running consequential work

Run a big job

Today you run a real, meaty piece of your work to done — staged your way, judged at your checkpoints.

Why one breath wanders

Hand over a whole project in one ask — "turn all this into a report" — and you force dozens of silent decisions: structure, emphasis, what to leave out. All made at once, all invisible until the end, when fixing means redoing. You'd never brief a colleague that way; you'd agree an outline first.

Checkpoints are where your judgment enters

Stage
one piece of the job
→
Checkpoint

→
Next stage

Cut the job into 3–6 pieces and place a checkpoint wherever a wrong guess would hurt — where your judgment matters, not where a template says. A classic chain: gather → outline → draft one section → review it → draft the rest → final pass.

At each checkpoint, a real verdict: specific praise or a specific change — "fine, continue" is not a verdict. Catch yourself about to wave a stage through? That pause is the skill. Cheap to correct here; expensive at the end.

Restart is the experienced move

Corrected the same problem twice and it's still drifting? Stop arguing. Failed attempts linger in a session's memory and pollute what comes next. The pro closes the task and starts fresh with a better brief that carries the lessons — not defeat, expertise.

You own what ships: anything that goes out under your name gets your check first — sized to the stakes.

Lesson 7 · your activity

Stage it, judge it, finish it

A real deliverable, done by the end — with your judgment at every gate.

  1. Pick the meaty task. A real multi-part deliverable from your delegation map — or one you name now: a report from scattered notes, a review over a messy folder. Big enough that one breath would disappoint; numbers in it? Even better.
  2. Stage it, 3–6 pieces. With your guide, write what you will judge at each checkpoint — your criteria, not just stage names. Put checkpoints where a wrong guess would hurt most.
  3. Break out and run it. + New task → Work in a project or folder → series-01-foundations. First move: Create job-plan.md with my stages and what I'll judge at each checkpoint — then let's start stage 1.
  4. Verdict at every checkpoint. Specific praise or a specific change, in your own words — about to type "ok"? Look again and find the one thing. Add a one-line verdict under each stage in job-plan.md as you go.
  5. If it drifts, restart with a better brief. Corrected the same thing twice? Close it, open a new task, and brief it better — carry the lessons, not the clutter.
  6. Finish it for real. Final pass, then your own last check: would you put your name on it? Then bring it home to debrief.
