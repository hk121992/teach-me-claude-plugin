Build your own machine

Nothing new to memorise today. You're going to use everything you built — to build one more thing, for your own work.

Two failure modes. You built both guards.

The two ways this technology quietly lets you down are siblings: flattery on the way in, confident nonsense on the way out — both fluent, both sure of themselves, both tilted away from truth. And without quite announcing it, you've already built the matched set that guards them:

Sycophancy
agrees with your worst idea
→
Input guard
your grill-me skill
Hallucination
wrong in a confident voice
→
Output guard
your verification checklist

You didn't collect tips. You built a matched set — one guard on what goes in, one on what comes out.

Pay for judgment once, keep the machine forever

A machine is anything that does a recurring task again without you re-thinking it: you spend your judgment once — on the brief, the shape, the checks — and every future run is nearly free. A machine doesn't have to be code. In rising order of power:

  1. A template — fill in the blanks, done.
  2. A checklist — the steps, in order, nothing forgotten.
  3. A formula — a spreadsheet does the arithmetic, right every time.
  4. A script the agent writes and runs — you never touch the code.
  5. A staged workflow — a repeatable job with your checkpoints built in.

Pick the lowest rung that does the job. A script where a template would do is over-build; hand-copying numbers a formula could fetch is under-build.

Lesson 10 · your build

Commission your machine

Audit → choose → grill → build → prove it on real cases. You leave with a machine you'll run next week.

  1. Run your audit — with your guide, before you build. What's working? What would you now do differently? No shame: the biggest "differently" is your build target.
  2. Choose from your map's capstone candidates — the task you flagged when you mapped your work. (No mark? Pick the recurring task that eats your week.)
  3. Start a new task (+ New task, top-left) → open Work in a project or folder → series-01-foundations. Bring the task's real material in — pause first for anything private, and copy what you can't afford to lose.
  4. Grill the brief. If you own the grill-me skill, turn it on yourself: Use my grill-me skill on this: I want a machine that … Sign the brief off before anything gets built.
  5. Pick the lowest rung that does the job — template, checklist, formula, script, workflow — and say why it's enough.
  6. Build. The agent does the building; you judge at every gate. Read what you approve before you approve it.
  7. Test on two real cases — one where you already know what good looks like. Open the outputs yourself; send it back in your own words until it's right.
  8. Write the how-to-use note in your own words — what it does, how to ask for a run, what to check after. Then book its first real run this week.
