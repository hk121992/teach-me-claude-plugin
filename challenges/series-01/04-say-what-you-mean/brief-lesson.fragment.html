Lesson 4 · the brief

Say what you mean

Not run the two-brief experiment yet? It's on the next slide — start there. This page lands harder once you've seen it with your own task.

Generic out usually means hollow in

Given a vague ask, the model doesn't stall — it fills every gap with its best plausible guess. Sometimes the guess is lucky. It is never dependably yours.

Generic output is usually a hollow brief, not a weak model. A specific brief is right every time; a vague one is right when you're lucky — and you can't tell from the outside which kind of lucky you got.

The four loads a brief carries

Context
what's going on that it can't see?
Audience
who reads this, and what do they care about?
Format
shape, length, structure, tone
Success criteria
what must be true to call it right?

Look back at your good brief from the experiment — you'll find most of these already in it, unlabelled. One of the four you naturally drop. That one is what your template is for.

Brief like a capable newcomer's day one

There are no magic keywords to hunt for. Brief the agent the way you'd brief a smart colleague who joined this morning: everything they'd need to get it right first time — nothing mystical. More relevant detail beats clever phrasing, every time.

Then make it repeatable: a template in your own words, saved in your kit, means next week's brief starts loaded instead of starting blank.

Lesson 4 · your activity

Brief it twice

Your own real writing task, briefed lazy and then loaded — same task, two fresh sessions. Watch what changes, then keep the good version's shape for good.

  1. Pick a real writing task from this week — an update, a tricky reply, an announcement. Anything in the material that isn't yours to share? Swap in a stand-in first.
  2. First send — the lazy ask. Click + New task (top-left) and don't pick a folder — this experiment needs no files. Send the laziest version you'd really send on a busy day, and don't polish it: Write an email about …
  3. Second send — the loaded ask. + New task again, same task: brief it the way you'd brief a capable newcomer on day one — what's going on, who'll read it, the shape you want, and what has to be true to call it right.
  4. Come back and name it. Your course chat is under Recents in the sidebar. Before your guide says anything, name two concrete differences in the text: The two answers differ in: 1) … 2) … — and the one I'd actually send is …
  5. Put names on the loads. With your guide, mark what your good brief carried — context, audience, format, success criteria — in your own words: which was missing from the lazy ask, and which one you tend to drop.
  6. Keep the shape. Distil your reusable template — 4–8 lines, your vocabulary — and save it to your kit: Save my brief template in my series folder as my-brief-template.md, with my good brief beside it as the worked example.

Take these with you

You'll be away from this page for the two sends — your guide will drop a link below to keep these beside you in your browser:
