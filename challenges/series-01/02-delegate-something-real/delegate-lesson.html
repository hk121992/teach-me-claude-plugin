Lesson 2 · your first real delegation

Delegate something real

Today the agent does an actual piece of your work — and you learn the three habits that make handing work over feel safe.

Pick the right first job

Not every task is a good first delegation. The best ones share three traits:

  • the input already exists — notes, a document, a messy draft
  • the output has a known shape — you could describe what "done" looks like
  • it's your own domain — you can judge the result at a glance

Choosing what to hand over — and what not to — is itself the skill. Delegation is a decision, not a reflex.

The first result is a draft for your judgment, not a verdict on your brief. Sending it back — "closer, but warmer" — is the normal move, not a failure. The work isn't done when the agent stops typing; it's done when you'd put your name on it.

Three habits make it safe

  • Copy before courage. Duplicate anything you can't afford to lose before the agent touches it. A copy makes every bold ask cheap — you can say yes to anything.
  • Read before you approve. When it's about to change or delete something, it says so and waits. Read that, say it back in plain words, then decide. Deleting, overwriting, moving — the actions you can't easily undo — get the most awake look. Resist "always allow" while you're learning.
  • Pause before you paste. Other people's private details, confidential material, passwords — check for them before real material goes in, and swap in a stand-in. (The full treatment comes later in the course.)

Lesson 2 · your activity

Hand over one real task

A real task from your week — you'll leave this lesson with it actually done.

  1. Start a new task (+ New task, top-left) and point it at your series folder via Work in a project or folder → series-01-foundations.
  2. Bring your material in — pause first. Anything private, confidential, or not yours to share? Swap it for a stand-in. Then copy the document into the folder.
  3. Copy before courage. Duplicate anything you can't afford to lose — e.g. Make a copy of report-notes.md called report-notes-backup.md before we start.
  4. Brief the job in your own words — what it is, who it's for, what good looks like. Like briefing a capable new colleague on day one.
  5. Read what it asks before you say yes. When it's about to act on a file, say the action back to yourself in plain words — then decide.
  6. Own the draft. Save a copy of version one, then ask for a bold revision in your own words — the copy makes it free. Compare, keep the best of both, and restore once from your copy so you know the way back.

Take these with you

You'll be in a new task, away from this page — your guide will drop a link below to keep these beside you:
