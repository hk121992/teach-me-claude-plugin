Lesson 2 · your first real delegation

Delegate something real

Today the agent drafts a real email from your work — and you learn the three habits that make handing work over feel safe.

Today's job: draft a real email

Not every task is a good first delegation — so today's is defined: an email you actually need to send, drafted with the agent. It has the three traits a good first delegation shares:

  • the input already exists — the thread you're answering, your notes, what it must say
  • the output has a known shape — an email; you know what "done" looks like
  • it's your own domain — you'll judge the result at a glance

You pick which email. Choosing what to hand over — and what not to — is itself the skill. Delegation is a decision, not a reflex.

The first result is a draft for your judgment, not a verdict on your brief. Sending it back — "closer, but warmer" — is the normal move, not a failure. The work isn't done when the agent stops typing; it's done when you'd put your name on it.

One honest expectation: it won't sound like you yet. Later in the course you'll learn how to get first drafts closer to your voice and style — for now, plan to iterate and review more. Editing it into your voice is part of the loop, not a failure of it.

Three habits make it safe

  • Version before the big change. Before a bold rewrite, keep the current version — v1 stays untouched while v2 takes the risk. Going back is just pointing at v1. A kept version makes every bold ask cheap — you can say yes to anything.
  • Read before you approve. You may get a permission request when it acts on files. Whenever one comes — or it proposes an action in chat — read it, say it back in plain words, then decide. Deleting, overwriting, moving — the actions you can't easily undo — get the most awake look. Resist "always allow" while you're learning.
  • Pause before you paste. Other people's private details, confidential material, passwords — check for them before real material goes in, and swap in a stand-in. For now we take a conservative risk posture; later in the course you'll learn how to decide what's safe to share in your own context.

Lesson 2 · your activity

Hand over one real email

A real email from your week — you'll leave this lesson with it drafted and ready to send.

  1. Pick the email — pause first. With your guide, before you go: a real email you need to send. Anything in the material that's private, confidential, or not yours to share? Swap it for a stand-in — conservative for now; your own sharing policy comes later in the course.
  2. Start a new task. In the Home tab, click + New (check Cowork is selected), then point it at your series folder via Project or folder → series-01-foundations.
  3. Paste the context in. The thread you're answering, your notes, what it must say — the agent drafts from what you give it. No email access needed.
  4. Brief the job in your own words — who it's for, what it must accomplish, tone and length. Like briefing a capable new colleague on day one.
  5. Read before you approve. You may get a permission request when it acts on files — read it, say it back to yourself in plain words, then decide.
  6. Own the draft — version it. e.g. Save this as draft-v1 and start a draft-v2 — keep v1 untouched. Then ask for one bold revision in your own words — v1 makes it free. Compare, keep the best of both — pull back anything you liked from v1. You send the final yourself.
