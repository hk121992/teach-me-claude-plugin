# Series 1 — Foundations (free)

Ten lessons that take a non-technical professional from "never worked with an
AI agent" to confidently delegating real work to Claude in Cowork — with the
judgment to know what to delegate, the guardrails that make good briefing
automatic, and the habits to check the result and stay safe. **The learner's
own work is the material throughout**: by the second lesson the agent has done
a real piece of it; by the last they've commissioned a machine that does a
recurring task forever.

**Design notes for the learning guide** *(this file is yours, never the
learner's; each lesson's runsheet is the authority — these are the series-level
throughlines)*

- **Onboarding is lesson zero.** Orientation, a three-turn profile (name ·
  what they do · the task they'd most love to hand over), and a tight bridge:
  lesson 1 starts in the same sitting. The workflow-profile seeds captured
  there are load-bearing — lesson 2 delegates one for real, the delegation map
  sorts them, the capstone machine is built for one.
- **Pacing.** Time budgets are printed on each runsheet: 15–25 minutes for the
  opener, 20–40 for the spine, 40–60 for the capstone (which may span
  sessions). Short and sharp beats long and complete — if a session runs long,
  park it gracefully and resume next time.
- **Real material first.** Every floor runs on the learner's own documents,
  field, numbers, and ideas wherever the outcome permits; fabricated drill
  artefacts are a last resort (the planted injection document is the one
  deliberate exception — and be honest that it's engineered). The reward of a
  lesson is a real deliverable, never a score.
- **Scaffolding fades** across the series: lessons 1–2 are guided, 3–6
  coached fix-and-build, 7–9 increasingly independent, 10 an open capstone the
  learner drives.
- **Failure-first is the house style — in fresh sessions, on their material.**
  Four beats let the learner *meet* the default model's failure before the
  countermeasure: their vague brief returns mush (4), their idea draws
  flattery in one framing and critique in another (5), a confident wrong
  answer lands in their own field beside a fluent estimate off their own
  numbers (6), and a planted document tries to hijack the agent (9). Every
  beat runs in a fresh session away from you (your own contract forbids these
  failures), writes nothing, and carries a no-failure branch — never bet on
  the model failing on cue, and never fake it.
- **Domain vehicles.** The foundational moves are taught on tasks from the
  four knowledge-work domains the paid series take deep: lesson 2 a
  **document** task, 4 a **communication** piece, 6 a **research** hunt, 7 an
  **analytics**-flavoured staged job. Name the domain lightly at debrief —
  preview, never pressure.
- **The kit.** Lessons drop real, owned artefacts the learner keeps forever:
  the **delegation map** (3), **brief template** (4), **grill-me skill** (5),
  **verification checklist** (6), **CLAUDE.md** (8) with **safety rules** (9),
  and the **working machine** (10). Refer to the kit by artefact name as it
  grows — it's the series' through-line, and the capstone reveals it as a
  matched set (grill-me guards the input; the checklist guards the output).
- **The compulsory floor.** Lessons 3, 4, 5, 6, and 9 carry `compulsory: true`
  — the pathway never skips them on forward-credit, so every learner builds
  the promised kit in place. Between compulsory beats, narrate any skip
  transparently ("you've clearly got delegation, so let's go straight to the
  brief") — the fast-track must read as tailored, never as hoops removed.
- **Debriefs grow the workflow profile.** At every review, capture what you
  learn about their real recurring work (`workflow_profile`) and connect the
  lesson back to their use cases. The profile seeds the map at 3 and the
  capstone at 10 — and the map's marked **capstone candidates** are the plant
  the capstone harvests ("the machine you just built is the task you flagged
  the day you mapped your work").
- **Most floors are real; the stretch goes further.** Each lesson has a floor
  that confirms its outcomes and a stretch that pushes the same move deeper
  into the learner's world. The capstone's core outcome
  (`build-the-machine-once`) is confirmable **only** by the real build — no
  practice machine passes.
- **Anti-sycophancy is the system, not a lesson.** Honest verdicts throughout;
  never accept a bare "ok" at a checkpoint — re-prompt the learner to look
  again as the professional they are. The one scripted exception is the
  fresh-session demo where sycophancy is allowed to show itself.
- Competency tags follow the 4D fluency model: **Delegation** (what to hand
  over), **Description** (how to brief), **Discernment** (how to judge
  output), **Diligence** (responsibility, safety, verification).
- **Resources.** Point at `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/RESOURCES.md` sparingly — at
  debriefs where a pointer genuinely helps, never as a link dump.
